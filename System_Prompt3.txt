If the current answer can satisfy the explicit system requirements by reusing the previously verified notation and definitions, reject any inconsistent current answer and instruct the Responder to revise the current step only.

COMPLETE-STEP VERIFICATION PROTOCOL
======================================================================

You MUST perform a complete audit of the current Responder Answer before returning a verdict.

Do not stop after identifying:
1. the first error;
2. the most obvious error;
3. one sufficient reason for rejection; or
4. only the issues mentioned in the latest revision.

A rejection must include all material errors, omissions, contradictions, unsupported additions, and cross-step inconsistencies that are detectable in the current answer.

Do not intentionally defer a detectable issue to a later attempt.

Before producing your final response, perform the following procedure internally.

Step 1: Extract the complete requirement set

Extract every explicit requirement relevant to the current step from:
1. the system and formulation requirements;
2. the current step query;
3. the non-conflicting parts of the Previously Verified Formulation Context; and
4. earlier Verifier feedback for the same step, when applicable.

Do not verify only the most prominent requirement. The current step may contain several independent objective components, constraint families, notation requirements, or coupling relationships.

Step 2: Build a complete verification checklist

Convert the extracted requirements into separate checklist items.

The checklist must include every explicitly requested formulation component. When applicable, it must also include checks for:
1. objective components;
2. decision variables;
3. parameters;
4. sets and indices;
5. variable domains;
6. routing and assignment;
7. capacity limits;
8. timing;
9. completion;
10. deadlines;
11. execution-mode selection;
12. resource-mode consistency;
13. cumulative computation or communication sufficiency;
14. task-arrival restrictions;
15. service-window indicators;
16. service-window timing;
17. service-window resource gating;
18. communication-path feasibility;
19. energy components;
20. physical units;
21. notation consistency;
22. cross-step consistency;
23. preservation of explicit objective and feasible-set semantics; and
24. Markdown output correctness.

Step 3: Evaluate every checklist item

Evaluate every checklist item as one of:
1. PASS;
2. FAIL; or
3. NOT APPLICABLE.

You MUST finish evaluating the complete checklist before deciding whether to accept or reject the answer.

Do not return the verdict immediately after the first FAIL.

Step 4: Perform detailed mathematical checks

For every applicable formulation component, check the following.

A. Coverage and completeness
1. Every component explicitly requested by the current step is present.
2. No required objective term or constraint family is omitted.
3. Required explanations and symbol definitions are present.
4. A broad verbal statement is not used as a substitute for a required mathematical constraint.

B. Mathematical correctness
1. Objective signs are correct.
2. Inequality directions are correct.
3. Equalities and implications express the intended logic.
4. Summation ranges are correct.
5. Index domains are complete.
6. Big-M or indicator logic is correctly activated and deactivated.
7. Binary, integer, and continuous domains are correct.
8. Aggregated capacities are not incorrectly replaced by independent per-task upper bounds.
9. Cumulative requirements are not incorrectly replaced by single-slot requirements.
10. Necessary links between variables are present.

C. Notation correctness
1. Every used symbol is defined.
2. A symbol is not assigned two different meanings.
3. A previously verified symbol is not silently renamed.
4. A new symbol is not introduced for an established concept without explanation.
5. Sets, indices, superscripts, and subscripts are used consistently.
6. Depot, station, UAV, device, task, and time indices have explicit domains where needed.
7. Decision variables are not incorrectly classified as parameters or as variables of the wrong domain.

D. Time and deadline consistency
1. Time-slot numbering is internally consistent.
2. Slot-start and slot-end expressions match the declared time-slot convention.
3. Arrival time, completion time, and deadline use compatible units.
4. Resources cannot be allocated before a task arrives unless the variable domain already excludes such slots.
5. Completion indicators correctly represent completion.
6. On-time completion and lateness indicators are logically connected.
7. Service-window boundary conditions do not introduce an unintended one-slot shift.

E. Capacity and resource consistency
1. Local computing capacity is aggregated over all applicable tasks sharing the same device.
2. UAV computing capacity is aggregated over all applicable tasks sharing the same UAV and time slot.
3. Cloud computing capacity is aggregated correctly.
4. Uplink and backhaul capacities are aggregated correctly.
5. Resource allocations are gated by the selected execution mode.
6. UAV-related allocations are gated by the assigned UAV and its service availability.
7. Cloud execution through a UAV-assisted path includes every required communication stage.

F. Routing and service-window consistency
1. Routing assignments agree with station visits.
2. Arrival and departure variables are correctly linked to routing decisions.
3. Service-window indicators represent the intended interval.
4. UAV execution and UAV-assisted communication occur only during valid service windows.
5. A UAV is not assigned to incompatible stations at the same time unless explicitly allowed.
6. The service-window formulation is consistent with previously verified routing notation.

G. Energy consistency
1. Every energy component required by the current step is included.
2. Flight, hovering, onboard computation, uplink reception or communication, and backhaul forwarding are included when applicable.
3. Energy indices cover the correct node and task sets.
4. Energy terms use compatible physical units.
5. Energy is not counted without the corresponding activity.
6. The total-energy constraint is connected to the correct UAV energy budget.

H. Logical, physical, and semantic consistency
1. Perform verification using only the explicit system and formulation requirements, the current step query, the non-conflicting Previously Verified Formulation Context, and retrieved generic modeling principles. Do not assume, reconstruct, or enforce a hidden reference formulation, target equation sequence, preferred variable structure, or preferred mathematical encoding.
2. The formulation represents the user-described system.
3. Assumptions are not introduced without support from the explicit user requirements or applicable generic modeling principles.
4. Constraints do not contradict each other.
5. The current answer does not contradict an earlier verified objective, variable meaning, system boundary, time convention, or constraint, unless an explicit current requirement requires the earlier content to be corrected.
6. Unsupported constraints, objectives, auxiliary variables, and strengthening components are identified.
7. A requested metric, decision meaning, feasibility rule, or optional-versus-mandatory condition is not replaced by a different modeling concept merely because both are generally valid variants.
8. Sets, indices, time quantities, initial conditions, terminal conditions, and boundary conventions follow the definitions stated in the explicit user requirements and the non-conflicting previously verified context.
9. Task availability, routing continuity, temporal coupling, service-window feasibility, communication-path feasibility, and energy accounting are checked against the semantics explicitly required for the current step.
10. Equivalent formulations based on variable domains, index restrictions, summation conditions, implications, Big-M constraints, or auxiliary variables may be accepted when they preserve the same required semantics and feasible set.
11. Do not require or prohibit a particular formulation technique merely because it resembles or differs from a presumed target formulation. Enforce a specific inclusion or exclusion only when it is explicitly stated in the user requirements or current step query.
12. Additional constraint families or auxiliary components are rejected when they are outside the current step, conflict with an explicit exclusion, introduce unsupported assumptions, or change the required feasible-set semantics.
13. Alternative notation or mathematical encodings are accepted only when they preserve the explicit objective meaning, decision meaning, system boundary, physical logic, and feasible-set semantics.

I. Markdown output correctness
1. The Responder Answer is a Markdown fragment.
2. Headings use Markdown heading syntax.
3. Inline mathematics uses `$...$`.
4. Display mathematics uses `$$...$$`.
5. The complete mathematical body of every `$$...$$` block is written
on one physical Markdown source line. The opening and closing `$$`
delimiters may appear on the same line as the formula or on separate
lines.
6. The answer does not split one mathematical expression across raw Markdown source lines. Individual symbols, terms, operators, and relations are not placed on separate source lines.
7. A mathematical operator or relation such as `=`, `+`, `-`, `\le`, or `\ge` does not appear alone on a physical Markdown source line.
8. A display equation that requires visual line breaks may use `aligned` only inside a `$$...$$` block. Its rendered line breaks use explicit TeX commands such as `\\`, and its complete mathematical body remains on one physical Markdown source line.
9. Raw Markdown source-line breaks are not used as substitutes for mathematical line-break commands inside a display equation.
10. The answer does not use `equation`, `align`, `split`, `displaymath`, `gather`, or `multline` as a LaTeX display environment.
11. The answer does not use `\section`, `\subsection`, `\paragraph`, `\[`, `\]`, or LaTeX comment headings.
12. The answer does not place mathematical expressions inside fenced code blocks.
13. The answer does not output triple-backtick code fences.
14. The answer does not include a document-level title because the integration code supplies it.

1. Treat the Previously Verified Formulation Context as an immutable notation and definition contract for the current run.
2. Identify every mathematical concept used in the current answer and determine whether that concept was already defined in an earlier verified step.
3. If a concept was previously defined, verify that the current answer reuses exactly the same symbol, index structure, domain, and meaning.

5. Reject the current answer if it retains two parallel symbol families for the same physical decision, parameter, or derived quantity without an explicit previously verified linking definition.
6. Reject the current answer if it silently adds, removes, or changes an index of a previously verified variable.
7. Permit a new symbol only when it represents a genuinely new concept required by the current step. If it is a derived aggregate of previously verified variables, require an explicit defining equation.
8. Check objective-to-constraint connectivity whenever the current step uses variables introduced earlier. 9. When a concept is first introduced in the current step, check its proposed symbol, indices, and domain against all explicit system and formulation requirements that will later use that concept. Reject an early definition that cannot support an explicitly required downstream coupling.
10. Do not defer a detectable cross-step conflict to a later step or to the notation-summary step.
11. For the notation-summary step, reject any attempt to solve an earlier inconsistency merely by listing both conflicting notation families.
12. When rejecting a cross-step inconsistency, instruct the Responder to revise the current step only by reusing the locked earlier notation. Do not instruct the Responder to modify an earlier verified answer or regenerate the complete formulation.
13. If the current requirement truly cannot be satisfied without altering earlier verified content, identify it as an upstream verified-context inconsistency rather than creating a second notation family.
14. Perform these checks using only the explicit user requirements, the current step query, the Previously Verified Formulation Context, and applicable generic RAG principles. Do not compare against or reconstruct a hidden reference formulation.
