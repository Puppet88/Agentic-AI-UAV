[System Description]

Consider a smart manufacturing system where a fleet of U homogeneous UAVs is dispatched from a central depot equipped with terrestrial mobile edge computing (MEC) servers, referred to as the cloud. The UAVs collect finished products from M geographically distributed manufacturing stations while simultaneously providing MEC services to industrial sensor devices (ISDs) at those stations.

The system follows a depot-based mission mode: depot → manufacturing stations → depot. UAVs fly at a fixed altitude and a constant given speed. Distances between routing nodes are given parameters. Travel time between two distinct routing nodes is determined from their given distance and the fixed UAV speed. Do not introduce position variables, trajectory-control variables, altitude-control variables, speed-optimization variables, or an additional wireless-channel model.

The formulation uses one given mission-horizon parameter. The discretized task-service interval contains T slots of duration Δ. The slot index set is {0, ..., T-1}. For slot t, the slot-start timestamp is tΔ and the slot-end timestamp is (t+1)Δ.

Use the same mission-horizon parameter as the UAV return-time upper bound and as the finite completion-time convention for a computational task that is missed or unfinished. Do not introduce separate routing and task-processing horizon parameters. Do not add an equation that defines the mission-horizon parameter as TΔ.

Service-window availability is evaluated at the slot-start timestamp. Completion in slot t is recorded at the slot-end timestamp.

Each station has a given product value and product weight. All finished products are ready for collection at the mission start. The requested formulation does not include station-specific product-release times, manufacturing-completion decisions, intermediate unloading, payload-reset decisions, or dynamic payload-state variables. Payload is accounted for over the complete UAV mission.

Task generation is stochastic at the system-operation level. However, the requested formulation is constructed over the realized set of tasks generated within the finite task-service interval. Each realized task is associated with one source ISD and one generation slot. Its arrival timestamp is the start timestamp of that generation slot. Treat the realized task set and its parameters as inputs to the formulation. Do not introduce expectations, scenario indices, chance constraints, task-arrival transition probabilities, or other stochastic-programming equation groups.

Each realized computational task has exactly one identifiable source ISD. Each source ISD is located at exactly one manufacturing station. Each task is associated with one generation slot. Its arrival timestamp is the start timestamp of that generation slot.

Each task has a computing workload, an input-data size, and a relative deadline duration determined by its source ISD. The task's absolute deadline is its arrival timestamp plus the relative deadline duration of that source ISD.

The formulation may use arrival-indexed task notation or a general task index. A general task index must preserve the task's source ISD, source station, generation slot, arrival timestamp, source-ISD-specific relative deadline duration, absolute deadline, workload, and input-data size.

Task flow time is measured from task arrival. For an on-time task, flow time is its recorded completion time minus its arrival timestamp. For a missed or unfinished task, flow time is the mission-horizon parameter minus its arrival timestamp.

Each task is executed through exactly one of three modes:
1. Local mode: The task is processed by its source ISD. This mode requires local computing service. It does not require UAV communication, UAV presence, UAV computing, backhaul, or cloud computing.
2. UAV mode: The task input is uploaded from the source ISD to one selected serving UAV. The task is processed by that same UAV. This mode requires uplink communication and UAV onboard computing through the same serving UAV. It does not require backhaul or cloud computing.
3. Cloud mode: One selected relay UAV receives the task input through the ISD-to-UAV uplink and forwards it through the UAV-to-cloud backhaul. The task is processed by the shared terrestrial cloud. The uplink and backhaul allocations use the same selected relay-UAV index.

For cloud mode, the full task input-data volume must be delivered through both the uplink hop and the backhaul hop. Do not introduce compression, expansion, partial-input transformation, or different required input-data volumes for the two communication hops.

Local computing capacity is defined separately for each ISD and is shared by all tasks generated by that ISD. It must not be replaced by a station-level local-computing capacity unless the system explicitly states that each station contains exactly one ISD.

UAV onboard computing, ISD-to-UAV uplink transmission, and UAV-to-cloud backhaul transmission require the corresponding UAV to be actively serving the task's source station.

Local computing does not require UAV presence. Cloud computing is performed by the terrestrial MEC server. Cloud-computing allocation is controlled by cloud-mode selection and the single shared cloud-computing capacity. It is not gated by the relay UAV's current presence at the source station. Do not introduce a service-window gate for terrestrial cloud computing.

Use cumulative computing and communication service sufficiency as the requested service-feasibility representation. Do not add separate data-buffer, data-arrival precedence, streaming-computation, processing-order, proportional-data, compression, or data-transformation constraint families.

Each UAV is constrained by payload capacity, maximum travel distance, the mission horizon, and a total energy budget covering flight energy, hovering energy, onboard-computing energy, and uplink/backhaul communication energy. Each station may be skipped and may be assigned to at most one UAV.

Station assignment determines the product weight carried by a UAV. Therefore, the full product weight of every station assigned to a UAV must be included in that UAV's mission-level payload. The requested formulation does not include a separate physical-pickup decision, a UAV-indexed actual-collection decision, a dynamic payload state, intermediate unloading, or payload reset.

Use one station-level product-collection-credit indicator for objective accounting. A station may receive collection credit only when it is assigned to a UAV. Assignment alone does not have to force the collection-credit indicator to one. The collection-credit indicator must not determine the payload carried by the UAV.

A UAV may remain unused. For each UAV, require the number of selected departures from the depot to equal the number of selected returns to the depot. Both counts may be zero. Do not introduce a separate single-tour activation variable or an at-most-one-departure restriction. Every selected route must satisfy the mission-time limit.

[Required Mathematical Formulation]

Please provide a complete and rigorous optimization problem formulation that jointly optimizes UAV routing, product collection assignment, task execution-mode selection (local / UAV / cloud), and computing and communication resource allocation.

Provide one complete formulation containing every mandatory equation group and modeling choice listed below. Every mandatory relation must preserve the stated mathematical and physical semantics.

Do not omit, replace, relax, or silently strengthen a mandatory relation. Do not add unrequested model variants, strengthening equation groups, auxiliary objectives, alternative resource models, stochastic-programming components, trajectory decisions, or additional physical assumptions.

Basic symbol definitions, index domains, binary and nonnegative variable domains, derived quantities, unit explanations, and concise explanatory text remain required. These items are not considered additional model variants.

Auxiliary variables, implication forms, Big-M forms, or other equivalent reformulations may be introduced only when they exactly represent an explicitly requested relation without changing its feasible-set semantics.

A restriction explicitly requested below, including exclusion of route self-loops, is part of the requested formulation and is not an unrequested strengthening.

Additional explanatory remarks and equivalent derived definitions are permitted when they clarify the mandatory formulation. They must not add a new objective term, decision family, physical assumption, or constraint family.

The formulation should cover:

1. All decision variables and parameters (including binary routing variables, timing variables, task execution mode indicators, resource allocation variables, and energy-related variables).

2. An objective function that maximizes total product-collection value and
the number of tasks completed within their deadlines. It must penalize
one binary deadline miss for every task that is not completed on time,
task flow time, and one combined normalized computing-and-communication
resource-occupation cost.

The deadline-miss cost depends only on whether the task is completed on time. Do not replace it with continuous tardiness or with the magnitude of lateness.

Task flow time is completion time minus the task-arrival timestamp. For a missed or unfinished task, use the mission-horizon parameter as its finite completion-time convention before subtracting its arrival timestamp.

Introduce one normalized computing-occupation quantity and one normalized communication-occupation quantity for each slot. Both quantities are dimensionless shorthand functions of the resource allocations in that slot. They are not independent decision variables and are not exogenous constants.

The computing-occupation quantity represents normalized occupation of the local-ISD, UAV, and shared-cloud computing resources. The cloud is one shared computing resource. Relay-UAV indexing of cloud allocations must not create several independent cloud resources or cause the same cloud allocation to be counted repeatedly.

The communication-occupation quantity represents normalized occupation of nominal uplink and backhaul allocations. Effective delivered rates must not replace nominal allocated rates in communication-occupation accounting.

Preserve the internal aggregation of these two quantities at this abstract derived-quantity level. Do not introduce a separate equation group that selects an average, weighted average, category-level average, system-wide average, peak-utilization rule, or another internal aggregation variant.

Do not replace normalized occupation with raw computing allocation, raw communication allocation, physical energy, or peak utilization. Sum the per-slot computing and communication occupation quantities over the slot set exactly once.

3. All constraints from UAV routing, organized as:
   (a) Collection assignment and route consistency constraints, including station assignment, station-level collection-credit linkage, incoming and outgoing station-flow consistency, and depot departure/return count balance. Use one station-level collection-credit indicator. A station may receive collection credit only when it is assigned to a UAV. Assignment alone does not have to force collection credit to one. Do not introduce a separate UAV-indexed physical-pickup or actual-collection decision. When a station is assigned to a UAV, the route-flow relations must give that UAV one selected incoming arc and one selected outgoing arc at that station. Define admissible route arcs only between distinct routing nodes. Exclude self-loops through the route-arc index domain. Do not add a separate self-loop equation group.
   (b) Payload capacity and travel-distance budget constraints. The payload of each UAV must include the full product weight of every station assigned to it. Payload accounting must use station assignment and must not depend on the optional collection-credit indicator. Use mission-level payload accounting. Do not introduce intermediate unloading, payload reset, route-dependent payload states, or dynamic payload-flow equations. Travel distance must use the given distances between routing nodes. Do not introduce trajectory, position, altitude, or speed optimization.
   (c) Mission-timing feasibility constraints. Fix every UAV's depot departure time at mission time zero. Require its return time to be no later than the mission-horizon parameter. Enforce travel-time consistency for selected depot-to-station, station-to-station, and station-to-depot arcs between distinct nodes. Calculate travel time from the given distance and fixed UAV speed. Require the minimum service duration at every assigned station. The route-flow and mission-timing constraints must jointly rule out disconnected station subtours. Do not add a separate subtour-elimination, MTZ, ordering-variable, commodity-flow, cut-set, or route-connectivity equation group. Require only depot departure/return count balance. Do not strengthen it through a separate single-tour activation variable or an at-most-one-departure restriction.

4. All constraints from task offloading, including:

   (a) Execution-mode selection and path identification. Require exactly one execution mode per realized task: local, UAV, or cloud. Local mode selects no UAV communication path. UAV mode identifies one serving UAV. The same UAV index must connect the selected mode with uplink allocation, UAV onboard-computing allocation, the applicable station service window, and UAV energy accounting. UAV mode does not use backhaul or cloud computing. Cloud mode identifies one relay UAV. The same relay-UAV index must connect the selected mode with uplink allocation, backhaul allocation, the service windows applicable to those two communication links, and UAV communication-energy accounting. Cloud computing uses one shared terrestrial cloud resource. The relay-UAV index identifies the communication path. It must not make terrestrial cloud-computing allocation dependent on the relay UAV's current presence at the source station. Uplink is required for UAV mode and cloud mode. Backhaul is required only for cloud mode. The full task input-data volume is required on every communication hop used by the selected mode.

   (b) Completion-time encoding. Use slot-selection binary variables together with an on-time-completion indicator. Each task may select zero or one valid on-time completion slot. A task's valid completion slots start at its generation slot. Completion in slot t is recorded at the slot-end timestamp (t+1)Δ. A task that does not select an on-time completion slot is treated as missed or unfinished. Do not introduce a separate completed-late state. Use the mission-horizon parameter as its finite completion-time convention.

   (c) Per-slot computing and communication capacity limits. Local computing capacity is defined separately for each source ISD. For each source ISD and each slot, aggregate the local-computing allocations of all realized tasks generated by that ISD whose generation slots are no later than the current slot. Compare that aggregate allocation with the capacity of the source ISD. Do not condition the local-capacity aggregation on whether a task has already completed. Do not introduce an active-task indicator, unfinished-task indicator, completion-dependent capacity-membership condition, or post-completion zero-allocation constraint. UAV onboard-computing capacity is defined separately for each UAV. For each UAV and each slot, aggregate the onboard-computing allocations of all realized tasks whose generation slots are no later than the current slot before applying that UAV's capacity. Cloud computing uses one shared cloud capacity. In each slot, aggregate all cloud-computing allocations over realized tasks and relay-UAV indices whose task-generation slots are no later than the current slot before applying the single shared capacity. Do not assign an independent copy of the cloud capacity to each relay UAV. Uplink and backhaul capacity limits are defined separately for each UAV. For each UAV and each slot, aggregate the corresponding nominal-rate allocations of all realized tasks whose generation slots are no later than the current slot before applying that UAV's communication-capacity limit. Use nominal allocated communication rates in these capacity limits.

   (d) Execution-mode consistency constraints. Local-computing allocation is zero unless local mode is selected. UAV-computing allocation is zero unless UAV mode is selected for that same UAV. Cloud-computing allocation indexed by a relay path is zero unless cloud mode is selected through that relay UAV. Uplink allocation is zero unless UAV mode or cloud mode is selected through that same UAV. Backhaul allocation is zero unless cloud mode is selected through that same relay UAV.

   (e) Multi-slot cumulative service-sufficiency constraints. Local mode requires sufficient cumulative local-computing service. UAV mode requires sufficient cumulative UAV-computing service and sufficient cumulative uplink data through the same serving UAV. Cloud mode requires sufficient cumulative cloud-computing service, sufficient cumulative uplink data, and sufficient cumulative backhaul data through the same relay UAV. Use the same full task input-data size for the uplink and backhaul requirements in cloud mode. Successfully delivered uplink and backhaul data are calculated from nominal allocated rates multiplied by the corresponding dimensionless effective-rate factors and slot duration. Use these cumulative constraints as the requested service-sufficiency representation. Do not add separate data-buffer, data-arrival precedence, streaming, compression, proportional-data, processing-order, active-task, unfinished-task, or post-completion zero-allocation constraint families.

   (f) Service-window indicator constraints. A UAV serves at most one station per slot. It may serve only an assigned station. A selected service slot is feasible only when its slot-start timestamp lies within the inclusive arrival-to-departure interval. Service availability must not require the entire slot interval to lie inside the service window. The service-window indicator provides one-way feasibility. Selecting a service slot requires its slot-start time to lie inside the continuous service interval. Do not require every slot inside that interval to be selected.

   (g) Service-window feasibility constraints. Apply the UAV-presence gate only to UAV onboard-computing allocation, ISD-to-UAV uplink allocation, and UAV-to-cloud backhaul allocation. These allocations must be zero unless the corresponding UAV is actively serving the task's source station in that slot. Local-computing allocation and terrestrial cloud-computing allocation must not be gated by the UAV service-window indicator.

   (h) Deadline satisfaction constraint. The relative deadline duration is associated with the task's source ISD. The absolute deadline is the generation-slot start timestamp plus that source-ISD-specific relative deadline duration. A task may be marked on time only if its recorded completion time is no later than this absolute deadline.

   (i) UAV energy budget constraint.
	Decompose UAV energy into flight energy, hovering energy, onboard-computing energy, and uplink/backhaul communication energy. Use the following energy semantics:
	- Flight energy is proportional to total selected route distance.
	- Hovering energy is proportional to the full continuous dwell duration at assigned stations.
	- UAV onboard-computing energy is proportional to allocated onboard computing work.
	- Uplink and backhaul communication energy are proportional to successfully delivered data volumes.

	Use the same effective delivered-data convention for communication sufficiency and communication energy. Do not replace this energy model with nonlinear propulsion, speed-dependent flight power, cubic DVFS computing energy, fixed per-task energy, or another energy-model variant. Do not calculate hovering energy solely from selected slot-level service indicators. Do not count the same dwell period through both continuous-time and slot-based hovering-energy terms.
